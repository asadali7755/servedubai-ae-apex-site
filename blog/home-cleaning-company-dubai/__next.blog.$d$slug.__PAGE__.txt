<h2>Insurance, Legal Compliance, and Safety Guarantees</h2>

<h3>UAE labor law requirements</h3>
<p>Every cleaning technician employed in the UAE must have a valid labor contract, a work permit (if applicable), and an Emirates ID. The law also mandates a minimum of 30 days’ paid annual leave and contributions to the General Pension and Social Security Authority. Ask the cleaning company to show you a compliance sheet that lists the number of staff on payroll, their contract status, and proof of pension contributions. This sheet reassures you that the team is legally hired and that you are not indirectly supporting undocumented labor.</p>
<h3>COVID‑19 hygiene protocols</h3>
<p>Since the pandemic, top‑tier cleaning firms have adopted a three‑step protocol:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Pre‑visit health screening</strong> – cleaners complete a digital questionnaire and temperature check before each job.</li>
<li><strong>EPA‑approved disinfectants</strong> – products that are effective against SARS‑CoV‑2 and safe for common home surfaces are used.</li>
<li><strong>Contact‑free service</strong> – cleaners wear disposable gloves and masks, and they place all used PPE in a sealed bag for removal after the job.</li>
</ol>
<p>Madinat Al Haya follows the Dubai Municipality’s "Safe Cleaning" guidelines, which require a minimum 15‑minute dwell time for disinfectants on high‑touch areas such as door handles, light switches, and remote controls. Request a copy of the cleaning checklist; it will show you exactly which products are used and the frequency of application.</p>
<h3>Liability and damage policies</h3>
